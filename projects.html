Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry for bacteria recognition

\( \mathcal{LCMS} \)
Abstract: Rapid identification of urinary pathogens from LC-MS data could shorten diagnosis time compared with standard culture methods. However, automatic classification is difficult due to high-dimensional signals, strong batch effects, and class imbalance. We study these challenges on LC-MS urine data collected across 13 batches and 28 bacterial classes, combining artificial samples with a limited set of real patient samples. We compare several deep learning approaches, including PCA, autoencoders, and encoder-based classifiers. We find that reconstruction-based latent spaces are not sufficient for accurate classification. In contrast, a simple batch correction--subtracting the mean blank signal per batch--combined with compact bottleneck classifiers trained for discrimination yields the most stable performance. The best model reaches 83.6% accuracy and 83.7 macro-F1 on the artificial samples. When applied to the real patient subset, the model achieves about 60% accuracy, showing that performance remains limited on clinical samples.
